# TD Diagrams & Beginner-Friendly Technical Communication Skill

This skill governs the standards for technical documentation, architectural diagrams, and pedagogical communication within the **Adaptive Defense for Multi-Agent Systems** project.

---

## 1. Diagram Formatting Protocol: Strict Top-Down (`TD`)

When authoring diagrams in Markdown artifacts or technical reports:

1. **Default to Top-Down (`TD`) Orientation:**
   - All Mermaid flowcharts and structural diagrams **must** use `flowchart TD` or `graph TD` by default.
   - Subgraphs should reinforce top-down data flow using `direction TB` where appropriate.
2. **Exception Criteria for Horizontal (`LR`) Layout:**
   - Only use `flowchart LR` when depicting a strictly linear, time-ordered sequence (e.g., a simple chronological pipeline $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow D$) where vertical stacking introduces excessive scrolling or compromises readability.
   - In all branching, multi-tier, hierarchical, or comparison diagrams, **always use `TD`**.
3. **Mermaid Syntax & Reliability Rules:**
   - Always quote node labels containing special characters, brackets, or math notation:
     ```mermaid
     flowchart TD
         A["Seed Input Graph G0"] --> B["Group Rollouts (K Candidates)"]
         B --> C["Reward Evaluator R(G')"]
     ```
   - Never embed raw HTML tags inside node text.
   - Use standard Mermaid diagram types: `flowchart TD`, `sequenceDiagram`, `stateDiagram-v2`, `classDiagram`.

## 2. Beginner-Friendly Pedagogical Guidelines

The primary researcher is learning advanced reinforcement learning, discrete adversarial optimization, and multi-agent systems. Explanations must bridge theoretical depth with clear intuitive models.

### A. The "Intuition First, Math Second" Rule
* Before presenting complex formulas, establish an intuitive real-world analogy or visual mental model.
* Example:
  > **Concept:** Bellman Error Compounding  
  > **Intuition:** Imagine trying to guess the score of a soccer game based on a single pass in the 10th minute. If your guess is slightly wrong, and you base your 15th-minute guess on your 10th-minute guess, your errors snowball until the prediction becomes completely detached from reality.

### B. Immediate Jargon Demystification
* Never use advanced RL or graph-theoretic terms without a 1-sentence plain-English definition:
  - **Policy ($\pi_\theta$):** The neural network making the decisions (e.g., generating text or choosing edges).
  - **Rollout ($o_i$):** A single completed trial or answer generated by the model.
  - **Advantage ($\hat{A}$):** A metric of how much better or worse an action was compared to an average baseline.
  - **Spectral Radius ($\rho$):** A mathematical measure of how easily information or errors amplify in an interconnected network.

## 3. High Density, Concise & To The Point

Maintain academic rigor while keeping responses compact and actionable:

1. **Eliminate Fluff:** Avoid verbose pleasantries, empty filler phrases, or repetitive re-summaries.
2. **Structure with Visual Anchors:** Use markdown comparison tables, bulleted lists, and alert blocks (`> [!NOTE]`, `> [!TIP]`, `> [!WARNING]`).
3. **Preserve Complete Details:** Do not simplify explanations by omitting equations, architectural components, or failure modes. Provide the complete mathematical formulation alongside the intuition.
